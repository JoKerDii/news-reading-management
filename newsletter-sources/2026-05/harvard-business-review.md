## Data Privacy Is a Growth Strategy
source: HBR Idea Watch · HBR Magazine — May/Jun 2026 (paywalled)
date: May–June 2026
url: https://hbr.org/2026/05/data-privacy-is-a-growth-strategy
tag: Policy

The piece reframes customer-data protection as a growth lever rather than a mere legal or IT obligation. Analyzing 360 corporate privacy announcements over 14 years, researchers found markets consistently rewarded firms that took privacy seriously, especially those with prior data breaches, and brand-level data linked strong privacy reputations to a roughly 12% rise in customer patronage. The recommendation: make privacy visible, proactive, and central to the customer experience.

## How to Build a Superteam That Keeps Getting Better
source: Ron Friedman · HBR Magazine — May/Jun 2026 (paywalled)
date: May–June 2026
url: https://hbr.org/2026/05/how-to-build-a-superteam-that-keeps-getting-better
tag: Funding

In fast-changing environments, the teams that outperform are those that learn fastest, not those with the best plans or most talent. Drawing on research across thousands of teams, Friedman identifies a consistent pattern among "superteams" and lays out seven practices: encourage experimentation even when things are going well, make curiosity and intellectual humility contagious, surface problems early, stay close to the work, give feedback that supports learning, and invest in people's growth. Tying work to shared meaning makes teams more resilient and adaptable.

## Negotiating When There Is No Plan B
source: Jonathan Hughes and Saptak Ray · HBR Magazine — May/Jun 2026 (paywalled)
date: May–June 2026
url: https://hbr.org/2026/05/negotiating-when-there-is-no-plan-b
tag: Leadership

The authors tackle how to find leverage when you lack a strong BATNA (best alternative to a negotiated agreement). The absence of a clear alternative does not mean powerlessness; negotiators can expand the concept of alternatives to include partial, temporary, and procedural options. Tactics such as identifying partial substitutes, finding hidden strengths, seeking tacit consent rather than explicit approval, reframing threats as warnings, and appealing to fairness can shift the dynamics even against "take it or leave it" ultimatums.

## Employees Are Relying on AI for Personal Support. That's Risky.
source: Constance Noonan Hadley and Sarah L. Wright · HBR Magazine — May/Jun 2026 (paywalled)
date: May–June 2026
url: https://hbr.org/2026/05/employees-are-relying-on-ai-for-personal-support-thats-risky
tag: Product

Workers increasingly turn to AI for career advice, emotional support, and even friendship. A survey of 1,545 U.S. knowledge workers found that more than half still felt lonely at work, a factor linked to lower job satisfaction and higher intent to quit, suggesting AI cannot replace human connection and may erode collaboration and social skills over time. The authors recommend five measures: monitor AI's social impact, set usage guidelines, design AI to foster human interaction, use it to organize social activities, and train employees in healthy use.

## Managing Difficult Directors
source: Marianna Zangrillo, Thomas Keil, and Stevo Pavićević · HBR Magazine — May/Jun 2026 (paywalled)
date: May–June 2026
url: https://hbr.org/2026/05/managing-difficult-directors
tag: Product

Boards often struggle because of one director's behavior rather than strategy or information gaps. The authors describe three types of difficult directors—passive passengers who stay silent, dominators who crowd out others, and misguided experts who mire the board in details—all of which slow decisions and erode trust. Effective boards set explicit expectations, give early and direct feedback, use structural levers like agenda design and speaking order, and escalate through formal governance processes when problems persist.

## The Rise of the Urban Knowledge Campus
source: Richard Florida, Masaki Hamura, Vladislav Boutenko, and Natalia Konyukova · HBR Magazine — May/Jun 2026 (paywalled)
date: May–June 2026
url: https://hbr.org/2026/05/the-rise-of-the-urban-knowledge-campus
tag: Product

Corporate headquarters are not vanishing but are being rebuilt as vehicles for collaboration, innovation, and talent attraction. Traditional office districts impose heavy costs through long commutes and fragmented logistics, whereas knowledge campuses integrate work, learning, socializing, and amenities within a single transit-linked place, improving productivity and life satisfaction. The authors outline the four key elements of a knowledge campus and offer principles for treating location as a driver of competitive advantage rather than a real-estate cost.

## Should You Appoint an Interim CEO?
source: Nicolas T. Deuschel, Robert Langan, and Christoph Mât · HBR Magazine — May/Jun 2026 (paywalled)
date: May–June 2026
url: https://hbr.org/2026/05/should-you-appoint-an-interim-ceo
tag: Crypto

A sudden CEO departure pressures boards to fill the void, and appointing an interim leader—now involved in one of three CEO transitions—often hurts market confidence and financial performance. Success depends on context: whether the departure was board- or CEO-initiated and whether the organization is stable or in crisis. The authors define four types of interim CEO suited to different situations and advise boards to build readiness into governance, clarify mandates, and secure workforce alignment for a successful permanent handover.

## What Companies Can Learn from Their Biggest Fans
source: Marcus Buckingham · HBR Magazine — May/Jun 2026 (paywalled)
date: May–June 2026
url: https://hbr.org/2026/05/what-companies-can-learn-from-their-biggest-fans
tag: Leadership

Despite decades of engagement efforts, employee trust and customer commitment keep declining because behavior shifts only when experiences are extremely positive—when people say they love them—not in response to mildly better ones. Drawing on large-scale research and examples such as Kroger and Disney, Buckingham shows outcomes accelerate only after experiences cross a critical emotional threshold. People are most likely to love an experience when it meets five conditions in sequence: control, harmony, significance, warmth, and growth.

## How Gen AI Robots Are Reshaping Services
source: Jochen Wirtz · HBR Magazine — May/Jun 2026 (paywalled)
date: May–June 2026
url: https://hbr.org/2026/05/how-gen-ai-robots-are-reshaping-services
tag: Crypto

Generative AI is turning robots from scripted machines into adaptive systems that interpret context, learn from demonstrations, and adjust behavior in real time, delivering personalized outcomes at scale. Examples like autonomous vehicles and humanoid factory assistants show robots handling complex instructions and collaborating with people. To deploy them successfully, companies should target use cases tied to real labor constraints, design natural interactions, position robots as partners rather than replacements, match capabilities to task variability, define success metrics, and keep privacy, transparency, and safety as priorities.

## Tapping into Your Team's Circadian Rhythms
source: Stefan Volk · HBR Magazine — May/Jun 2026 (paywalled)
date: May–June 2026
url: https://hbr.org/2026/05/tapping-into-your-teams-circadian-rhythms
tag: Leadership

Corporate culture has favored "morningness," but people have different, hard-to-change circadian rhythms that cause daily fluctuations in peak functioning. Leaders should schedule strategic thinking, feedback, and emotionally demanding work during high-energy periods and delegate or take lower-intensity roles during low-energy ones. For teams, schedule high-stakes collaboration during shared peaks, assign challenging tasks to individuals' peaks and routine work to off-peaks, and when alignment isn't possible, prioritize recovery, flexibility, and rotation to prevent burnout.

## The CEO of UnitedHealthcare on Fixing What Frustrates Customers Most
source: Tim Noel · HBR Magazine — May/Jun 2026 (paywalled)
date: May–June 2026
url: https://hbr.org/2026/05/the-ceo-of-unitedhealthcare-on-fixing-what-frustrates-customers-most
tag: Leadership

Noel describes UnitedHealthcare's Consumer Resolution Center, which combines AI insights with human judgment to proactively identify customers in distress and empower skilled employees to resolve their problems quickly. Working across functions with minimal bureaucracy, CRC teams turned service recovery into a source of organizational learning that let the company rewire processes, policies, and benefits at scale. The story illustrates staying close to frontline reality, trusting data over intuition, and moving decisively to scale what works.

## Case Study: Should a Funeral Company Shift Its Business Model?
source: Andres Cuneo, Marco Bertini, and Jill Avery · HBR Magazine — May/Jun 2026 (paywalled)
date: May–June 2026
url: https://hbr.org/2026/05/case-study-should-a-funeral-company-shift-its-business-model
tag: Funding

Javier Montoya, the 76-year-old CEO of Chile's Monteverde Memorial Group, faces a strategic inflection point for his burial-park business, which has long prospered by selling plots to a growing middle class. Rising land and water costs, new environmental regulations, and shifting consumer preferences accelerated by Covid are slowing expansion while fueling growth in lower-margin, commoditized cremation. Weighing financial trade-offs against his personal convictions and competing advice, Javier must decide whether to keep investing in memorial parks or pivot toward cremation.
