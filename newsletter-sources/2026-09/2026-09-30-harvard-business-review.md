### 2. [For Real Performance Gains with AI, Start by Mapping Your Workflows](https://hbr.org/2026/11/for-real-performance-gains-with-ai-start-by-mapping-your-workflows)
**Published:** November–December 2026 | **By:** Author not listed · HBR Magazine — Nov/Dec 2026
**Summary:** Companies struggle to identify where AI can produce meaningful gains, a challenge researchers from INSEAD and Harvard Business School call the "mapping problem." In a randomized experiment with 515 early-stage firms, those shown examples of how others redesigned work around AI found 44% more use cases, completed 12% more tasks, and generated nearly double the revenue. The article recommends mapping workflows, redesigning whole processes, and letting people experiment.

### 5. [Design Your Supply Chain as a Portfolio of Options](https://hbr.org/2026/11/design-your-supply-chain-as-a-portfolio-of-options)
**Published:** November–December 2026 | **By:** Luiz Mesquita, Roberto Vassolo, Jean-Paul Destarac, and Thomas Y. Choi · HBR Magazine — Nov/Dec 2026
**Summary:** Tariffs, export controls, sanctions, and shifting alliances make traditional resilience tactics like buffer inventories and supplier diversification insufficient. Drawing on real-options theory and their consulting work with global manufacturers, the authors present a framework built on four options: defer, abandon, expand, and shift operations. They advise leaders to reframe how supply chain investments are evaluated, audit their commitment profile, and build long-lead capabilities early.

### 6. [How CEOs Set the Tempo](https://hbr.org/2026/11/how-ceos-set-the-tempo)
**Published:** November–December 2026 | **By:** Nitin Nohria · HBR Magazine — Nov/Dec 2026
**Summary:** A CEO sets an organization's pace of decisions and change, and the author argues that wrong tempo, not wrong strategy, often costs executives their jobs. Drawing on observation of several hundred CEOs, he describes five tempos: experimental speed, relentless pressure, disciplined urgency, front-loaded deliberation, and patient compounding. The right choice depends on the leader's temperament, company life stage, business challenge, internal readiness, and external pressures.

### 7. [Do You Know What Your Customers Value?](https://hbr.org/2026/11/do-you-know-what-your-customers-value)
**Published:** November–December 2026 | **By:** Vikas Mittal, Alessandro Piazza, Sonam Singh, and Ashwin Malshe · HBR Magazine — Nov/Dec 2026
**Summary:** Executives who invest heavily in customer feedback and competitor monitoring often believe they know what customers want. A seven-year study of 72 companies found that many prioritized the wrong value drivers or kept switching priorities, relying on salient information and unsupported assumptions. The authors propose a five-step fix: surface misalignment, assess each driver's lift potential, embed drivers in daily work, cut non-value-adding activities, and avoid marketing strengths customers do not value.

### 10. [Building a Board That Fits](https://hbr.org/2026/11/building-a-board-that-fits)
**Published:** November–December 2026 | **By:** Timothy J. Rowley and Stanislav Shekshnia · HBR Magazine — Nov/Dec 2026

### 11. [The New Platform Playbook](https://hbr.org/2026/11/the-new-platform-playbook)
**Published:** November–December 2026 | **By:** Chiara Farronato · HBR Magazine — Nov/Dec 2026
**Summary:** For two decades platform strategy followed a formula of rapid scale, network effects, and winner-take-all dominance. The author argues the playbook is outdated as tighter regulation and other forces reshape the game, even though network effects still matter. She proposes four growth strategies: prioritize users who create the greatest spillovers, make the market thicker for current users, look beyond obvious monetization, and treat customer welfare as strategy.

### 12. [How Leaders Counter Pessimism in Trying Times](https://hbr.org/2026/11/how-leaders-counter-pessimism-in-trying-times)
**Published:** November–December 2026 | **By:** Michelle Gielan · HBR Magazine — Nov/Dec 2026
**Summary:** When teams face negative news, failures, or uncertainty, learned helplessness and pessimism can erode engagement, energy, and performance, and forced positivity does not remedy it. The author recommends leaders model constructive behavior around attention, prioritization, and restoration; reframe stress with a signal check and a "Yes, and" response; and create positives through a three-day sequence of sending praise, lightening a load, and reaching back out.
