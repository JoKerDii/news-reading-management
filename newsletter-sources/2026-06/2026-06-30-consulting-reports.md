## Boston Consulting Group (BCG)

### 1. [From Recovery to Resurgence in Global Fintech (Annual Report)](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/from-recovery-to-resurgence-in-global-fintech)

**Published:** 2026-06-01 | **By:** BCG and FT Partners

**Summary:** The fourth annual BCG–FT Partners fintech report finds global fintech revenues surpassed half a trillion dollars in 2025, growing 22% year over year, more than four times the rate of incumbent financial firms. Some 74% of the largest public fintechs are now profitable, equity funding rose 53% to $58 billion, and IPOs climbed 50%. The report concludes fintech has matured into a distinct sector, with AI-effective firms achieving up to five times greater developer productivity.

### 2. [AI at Work: Why Strategy Matters More Than Tools (Global Survey)](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/ai-at-work-why-strategy-matters-more-than-tools)

**Published:** 2026-06-02 | **By:** BCG

**Summary:** BCG's global workforce survey finds AI is changing jobs faster than companies are redesigning operations to keep pace, making strategic clarity the decisive factor in capturing value. Among frontline employees who regularly use AI, 42% report saving at least a workday per week. The report argues that leadership direction and process redesign, rather than the tools themselves, separate organizations realizing returns from those stuck in experimentation.

### 3. [AI for CEOs: Amplifying Time and Judgment at the Top](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/ai-for-ceos)

**Published:** 2026-06-23 | **By:** BCG

**Summary:** BCG argues that AI tailored to an individual chief executive can amplify a company's two scarcest resources: the CEO's time and judgment. Its 2026 research finds CEOs who spend at least eight hours a week building AI capabilities are more likely to generate meaningful value, with leading CEOs adopting customized agentic systems built around their decision patterns. The report cautions against AI-influenced groupthink and cognitive burnout and offers five questions to assess whether AI is improving judgment.

## Bain & Company

### 1. [Private Equity Midyear Report 2026: Control the Controllable, Weather the Rest](https://www.bain.com/insights/private-equity-midyear-report-2026/)

**Published:** 2026-06-08 | **By:** Bain & Company

**Summary:** Bain's midyear report finds the private equity recovery that built momentum in early 2026 has stalled amid three rapid shocks: an AI-driven "SaaSpocalypse" in software, redemption stress in private credit, and an oil-price spike tied to conflict with Iran. Tech deal value fell roughly 70% from Q4 2025 to Q1 2026. Bain argues winning firms should focus on what they can control, including value creation and disciplined AI adoption, while noting markets remain fundamentally sound with abundant dry powder.

## PwC (PricewaterhouseCoopers)

### 1. [PwC 2026 Global AI Jobs Barometer](https://www.pwc.com/gx/en/news-room/press-releases/2026/pwc-2026-ai-jobs-barometer.html)

**Published:** 2026-06-15 | **By:** PwC

**Summary:** Analyzing more than one billion job ads across 27 countries, PwC finds AI is splitting the labor market into two tracks: "professionalised" roles, where AI amplifies expert judgment, are seeing roughly twice the job growth and 42% faster wage growth than "democratised" roles. The AI-skills wage premium rose to 62%, and the most AI-exposed companies grew headcount faster than the least exposed (52% versus 36%). Entry-level AI-exposed roles increasingly demand senior, human-centric skills such as judgment and leadership.

**Summary:** EY's survey of 1,610 CFOs and finance directors across 28 countries finds many finance chiefs are not consistently owning decisions with uncertain or long-term payoffs, often defaulting to safer short-term choices.
