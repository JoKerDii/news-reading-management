### 2. [The False Alignment Trap](https://hbr.org/2026/07/the-false-alignment-trap)

**Published:** July–August 2026 | **By:** Julia Dhar, Kristy R. Ellmer, Philip Jameson · HBR Magazine — Jul/Aug 2026

**Summary:** The authors, all from BCG, argue that most transformations fail not from poor execution but from "false alignment"—senior leaders assuming they agree on why, what, and how to change when they actually do not. Drawing on research into nearly 2,000 companies, they contrast this surface-level alignment with "true agreement," in which leaders forge detailed, explicit compacts that let them pursue shared priorities and hold one another accountable. The piece offers ways to surface hidden disagreement before launching change.

### 3. [How the Best Leaders Respond to Rule Breaking](https://hbr.org/2026/07/how-the-best-leaders-respond-to-rule-breaking)

**Published:** July–August 2026 | **By:** Michael J. Gill · HBR Magazine — Jul/Aug 2026

**Summary:** Synthesizing more than 250 studies across four decades, Oxford's Michael J. Gill shows that employees break rules for very different reasons—self-interested, prosocial, corrupted, and situational—and that reflexive punishment is often the wrong response. He argues the best leaders diagnose intent and look for patterns, since repeated violations frequently signal an unrealistic, unclear, or misaligned policy. Recognizing good intentions, even when outcomes fall short, encourages transparency and smarter risk-taking.

### 4. [What Companies Get Wrong About Decision Rights](https://hbr.org/2026/07/what-companies-get-wrong-about-decision-rights)

**Published:** July–August 2026 | **By:** Lindy Greer, Jennifer Jordan, Maxim Sytch · HBR Magazine — Jul/Aug 2026

**Summary:** Tools like RACI are meant to clarify who provides input on a decision, who makes it, and who executes it, but they often fail in practice. The authors identify four common mistakes: confirming roles without clarifying goals, assuming everyone will adhere to the boss's spreadsheet, misunderstanding roles, and getting stuck in the same roles. They recommend an ARCI variant that puts the decision owner first and ensuring only one person is accountable for any given decision to prevent power struggles and delays.

**Published:** July–August 2026 | **By:** Rita McGrath · HBR Magazine — Jul/Aug 2026

**Summary:** In a "dematerializing economy" where value has shifted from physical assets to intangibles like data, software, and capabilities, McGrath argues that traditional strategy frameworks no longer suffice. ### 10. [Bring Back Managing for Value](https://hbr.org/2026/07/bring-back-managing-for-value)

**Published:** July–August 2026 | **By:** Michael Mankins, Matthew Crupi · HBR Magazine — Jul/Aug 2026

**Summary:** After two decades in which managing for shareholder value fell out of favor in favor of stakeholder- and purpose-driven models, the authors argue that a less forgiving capital-market environment makes value-based management essential again. **Summary:** Studying 11 successful coaches in the NFL, NBA, and other elite leagues, the authors distill how top coaches decide under compressed time frames and intense public scrutiny.
