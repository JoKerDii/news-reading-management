### 4. [Why Even Your Best Employees Override AI Systems That Work](https://hbr.org/2026/11/why-even-your-best-employees-override-ai-systems-that-work)
**Published:** November–December 2026 | **By:** Das Narayandas · HBR Magazine — Nov/Dec 2026
**Summary:** Even reliable AI systems are often overridden by the people meant to use them. Drawing on an example of a global bank whose credit-decisioning system performed well in pilots but still sent most files to human review, the article argues the cause is not distrust of the technology but distrust of the organization. It points to unclear authority and accountability as the root problem.

### 5. [Design Your Supply Chain as a Portfolio of Options](https://hbr.org/2026/11/design-your-supply-chain-as-a-portfolio-of-options)
**Published:** November–December 2026 | **By:** Luiz Mesquita, Roberto Vassolo, Jean-Paul Destarac, Thomas Y. Choi · HBR Magazine — Nov/Dec 2026
**Summary:** Tariff wars, export controls on critical inputs, shifting geopolitical alignments, and conflicts have made volatility the rule for global supply chains, while most planning tools assume it is the exception. The authors propose treating the supply chain as a portfolio of options. Companies should be able to defer investments, quit unfavorable positions, expand in promising regions, and shift operations as policies evolve.
